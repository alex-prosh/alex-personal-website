# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Academic peers (professors, PhD programs, collaborators) and industry robotics recruiters/engineers. They are technical, busy, and allergic to marketing. They arrive via a paper citation, a Twitter link, or a Google search for the author's name.

## Product Purpose

Personal research website for a robotics PhD researcher at UC Berkeley. Exists to establish credibility, surface work, and be the canonical link in paper footers and social bios. Success means someone can find a paper, understand the research area, and read a blog post without feeling like they landed on a template.

## Brand Personality

Curious, Sharp, Human. Not performing brilliance — genuinely engaged. The work is the point; the site is just where it lives.

## Anti-references

Generic academic lab pages (ugly, unreadable, 2005-era). Over-designed portfolio sites with big hero animations and "crafted with love" footers. AI-generated warm-brown card grids. Startup-style SaaS personal sites.

## Design Principles

1. Content leads. The site should feel like a well-typeset document, not a designed product.
2. Trust through restraint. No color you don't need. No spacing that doesn't earn its place.
3. The researcher's voice, not a brand voice. Copy should sound like a person, not a profile.
4. References over claims. Show the work; don't describe it.
5. Fast everywhere. A researcher's site should load instantly on a conference hotel WiFi.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. No motion required for any interaction. Readable at 150% zoom.
